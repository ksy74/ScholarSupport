About

Education doesn't stop at the classroom door.

That's the idea behind our health literacy campaign, and why it exists alongside, not instead of, ScholarSupport's core work.

The problem

In many under-resourced and rural communities, health information is either hard to find, written above the reading level people are comfortable with, or simply not available in the languages people speak at home. That gap has real consequences: preventable illnesses go unrecognized, and basic questions about nutrition, hygiene, and care go unanswered.

These are often the same communities where students in our education programs are learning, so the need was hard to miss.

Our approach

We keep it simple and direct: plain-language health lessons that don't require a medical background to understand, in-person workshops led by students and local volunteers, and food and basic resource support delivered alongside the lessons, because it's hard to focus on a health lesson on an empty stomach.

Everything we produce is written to roughly an 8th-grade reading level, and we prioritize topics that communities tell us they actually want to know more about.

Connection to ScholarSupport

ScholarSupport is a student-run initiative focused on supporting under-resourced learners. This health literacy campaign grew directly out of that work: the same communities we support academically often lack access to basic health information, food, water, and books. So alongside tutoring and academic support, we now bring health lessons and essential resources to the families we already work with.

This campaign is run in partnership with the KSNV Foundation.

From our founder

Yashasvi Kompella
Co-Founder, ScholarSupport

I am passionate about health access and literacy for the next generation of scholars, students in under-resourced and rural communities who have real ambition but not always the resources to match it. I started this campaign because I believe a lack of health knowledge, food, or basic supplies should never be the reason a capable student falls behind, and I am committed to closing that gap one lesson, one workshop, and one meal at a time.
